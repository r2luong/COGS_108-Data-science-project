## Abstract

This project investigates how reviewer behavior varies among critical, generous, and neutral Google Maps users in California. Our project focuses only on establishments that are highly-rated (≥ 100 reviews and average rating > 4.5) or harshly-rated (≥ 100 reviews and average rating < 3.5>). We classify reviewers based on whether they consistently overrate or under-rate these specific businesses. Our research question asks: How do review length, average star rating, review frequency, geographic diversity, and sentiment differ across these three user groups? Using a combined dataset consisting of approximately 44 million reviews and approximately 516,000 businesses, we explore rating tendencies, reviewing patterns, textual features, and sentiment scores. This analysis provides data-driven insights into behavioral differences across the types of reviewers. To answer our question, we capture results such as user metrics(mean of ratings, variance, etc.), text characteristics(review length and vocabulary), geographical information, and sentiment scores. Additionally, our project uses dimensionality reduction to visualize reviewer statistics with multiple dimensions, as well as classification to see how well we can classify reviewers. In essence, our study involves numerous methods to examine reviewer behaviors and how they shape the rating patterns of establishments.
